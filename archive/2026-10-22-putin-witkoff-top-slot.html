Zelenskyy says Russia fired three cluster-warhead ballistic missiles at Kyiv after the Putin–Witkoff talks

Latest Ukrinform · Thu 9:15 a.m.First reported by PBS NewsHour (The Associated Press) · Wed 9:15 a.m.

Russia fired three ballistic missiles with cluster warheads at Kyiv overnight, one striking Pechersk, the historic centre, between the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra and the Holy Vvedensky Monastery, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says: “a deliberate choice of weapon, a deliberate choice of target area” (Ukrinform). No decision has been made on who will represent Russia at the planned three-way talks, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov says (Reuters via A News). A strike on a market in Lozova, Kharkiv region, killed two women and injured 34, Governor Oleh Syniehubov says (UNN).

  • 3 ballistic missiles with cluster warheads were among those fired at Kyiv overnight, Zelenskyy says; he also counts more than 170 strikes on critical energy infrastructure since Oct. 11 (Ukrinform; Mezha)
  • At least 2 killed in a Russian strike on a market in Lozova, Kharkiv region, Governor Oleh Synehubov says (Interfax-Ukraine); 34 injured and the two killed were women aged 50 and 60, he now says (UNN; earlier 17, The New Voice of Ukraine); confirmed by Interfax-Ukraine
  • 21 ballistic missiles fired at Kyiv in three waves overnight; 12 regions attacked and 34 wounded, including five children, Zelenskyy says (Kyiv Post)
  • At least 9 killed and 56 injured across Ukraine over the past day: 3 in the Zaporizhzhia region (Governor Ivan Fedorov), 2 in Donetsk (Governor Vadym Filashkin), 1 each in Kyiv, Sumy, Chernihiv and the Kharkiv region, where 15 were injured, including three children (Governor Oleh Syniehubov) (Kyiv Independent)
  • 1 killed, 24 injured in Kyiv, 16 of them in hospital, including a 16-year-old boy with a brain injury; 13 private homes destroyed in the Dniprovskyi district, according to the Kyiv City Military Administration and prosecutors (Mezha; The Associated Press)
  • Nov. 10 after which Putin agreed “in principle” to resume trilateral talks, Ushakov says (The Associated Press via CTV News)

  • The exact date and venue: Zelenskyy says he does not know the final dates yet and expects the meeting by early November at the latest (Ukrinform); whether he will take part himself; he says only a leaders’ summit can break the impasse (The Associated Press via CTV News)
  • Whether a Trump–Putin–Xi meeting, which Russian officials call “possible,” goes ahead; Ukrainian officials have balked at it (CBS News); Ushakov says a Putin–Trump–Xi summit “was also being prepared in China’s Shenzhen for next month,” and that the European and Ukrainian proposals Witkoff brought lacked substance: “there wasn’t really much substance in them” (AFP via The Manila Times)

What we know

  • The State Department says the release is “a welcome step that we hope leads to the release of all Americans unjustly held in Russia” and that “there are currently no Americans determined to be wrongfully detained in Russia.” Hubbard’s son, Joseph Coleman, an Orthodox priest: “As I await the blessing of embracing him again, I receive his freedom not as a political victory, but as a gift of mercy.” (BBC News)
  • Putin agreed “in principle” to resume trilateral talks after Nov. 10, Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said after the St. Petersburg meeting (The Associated Press via CTV News).
  • Russia released American Stephen Hubbard; Trump says no swap was needed (CBS News). Hubbard, 74, from Michigan, had lived in Izyum, eastern Ukraine, since 2014; his lawyer Martin De Luca asks for privacy “as Stephen recovers and reconnects with the people who love him” (NBC News).
  • Hubbard’s sister says he was in perfect health when he was taken in 2022 and now has an enlarged heart and likely post-traumatic stress disorder; she has slept on her couch for four years in solidarity with him, “because I figured he didn’t have a bed. Why should I have a bed?” (The Associated Press via KSAT San Antonio)
  • Hubbard’s sister, Patricia Hubbard Fox, thanked Trump “for being the hand of God and bringing Stephen Hubbard home”; she says she learned of the release from the media; he landed overnight at Joint Base San Antonio-Lackland (The Associated Press via KSAT San Antonio); CNN reports he goes to Washington first to see his family, then to a military facility in Texas (CNN; ABC News).
  • Hubbard grew up in Big Rapids, Mich., served in the U.S. Air Force and National Guard and taught English in Japan for 25 years before moving to Izyum in 2014 (FOX 2 Detroit, citing Reuters and The New York Times). He once taught in Pueblo, Colo., and his sister Patricia lives in Chaffee County; she says she was never allowed to speak with him while he was imprisoned (KRDO).
  • Ushakov says the talks must focus on “building a security system for Russia and Ukraine after the end of the conflict” (BBC News); Trump says sanctions would have brought the talks that freed Hubbard to “a complete and total halt” (ABC News).
  • Zelenskyy told Fox News that freezing the conflict is the “minimum” needed to end the war (The Associated Press via CTV News).
  • At a roundtable in Washington on Wednesday, Trump said a deal to “settle the war” is “maybe now the closest we’ve been” (Kyiv Independent).

Not yet known

  • An exact date and who will sit at the table; Ushakov told Russian state TV the sides agreed on Abu Dhabi around mid-November (Reuters via Investing.com; TASS); Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov says Thursday “at this point, we are talking about Abu Dhabi” (TASS).
  • What was in the proposals Witkoff brought, which the Kremlin attributes to Ukraine and the EU (CBS News).
