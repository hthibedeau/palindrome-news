Sutcliffe vows $95 Ottawa transit pass in 2027 budget as Ontario’s new mayors get to work

Latest CTV News · Tue 7:22 p.m.First reported by CP24 and CTV News · Mon 9:59 p.m.

In Waterloo region, six of seven mayoral seats changed hands on Monday and one regional council seat was decided by three votes; the province says it will appoint regional chairs within 30 to 40 days (CTV News). Ottawa Mayor Mark Sutcliffe, back at work a day after winning a second term, says a $95 monthly transit pass will be in the 2027 budget “whether the upload is completed by that time or not,” and that he won’t use strong-mayor powers (CTV News). In Cambridge, mayor-elect Scott Hamilton, who unseated Jan Liggett with nearly 57 per cent, promises “a fresh start” (CTV News). At least two dozen Ontario towns and cities voted for change on Monday; pollster Nik Nanos calls it “a bit of a warning shot” (The Canadian Press via CTV News).

  • 24+ Ontario towns and cities voted for change on Monday, by The Canadian Press’s count; Ottawa’s Mark Sutcliffe and Brampton’s Patrick Brown were re-elected (The Canadian Press via CTV News)
  • $1.1 billion projected Toronto budget shortfall for 2027; the 2023 provincial deal worth $1.2 billion in operating funds expires this year and talks with Ontario are ongoing (The Canadian Press via CKOM)
  • 532,201 votes for Chow to 404,013 for Bradford as of Tuesday morning (CP24); the City of Toronto’s unofficial results showed 529,042 (54.17%) to 401,415 (41.11%) with 1,420 of 1,431 polls in (City of Toronto)
  • 45.8% Toronto turnout, 982,660 ballots at all 1,431 polls, the highest since 54.7 per cent in 2014 (unofficial) (CityNews)
  • 74,479 votes (39.2%) for Tedjo in Mississauga, to 51,853 (27.3%) for Parrish and 43,191 (22.7%) for Bonnie Crombie (CBC News)
  • 41.77% for Keanin Loomis in Hamilton, to 31.87% for Coun. Rob Cooper and 14.53% for Mayor Andrea Horwath; eight new councillors, and turnout of 31.59%, down from 35.58%, unofficial results show (CBC News)
  • 16,112 votes for Minakakis in Milton to 8,878 for Krantz, mayor since 1980, with 31 of 38 polls reporting (insauga)
  • 44.6% for Sutcliffe in Ottawa to 33.9% for Jeff Leiper (CTV News); turnout 36.21%, the lowest since 2003, the city reports; Sutcliffe vows a $95 monthly transit pass in the 2027 budget (CTV News; CTV News)
  • Kingston’s official results, which the city says will be posted Tuesday; unofficial results give Conny Glenn 43.25 per cent to 41.17 for Mayor Bryan Paterson, a 793-vote margin (The Queen’s Journal); Glenn’s term begins when the new council is sworn in Nov. 15 (The Queen’s Journal)
  • Final results: Toronto’s are unofficial, and the City Clerk is expected to certify them later in the week (Global News)

What we know

  • CP24 and CTV News were first to declare Chow’s victory, at 9:59 p.m. “Whether you voted for me or voted for someone else … I am your mayor too,” she said (CTV News). Toronto is “a progressive city, a city of compassion, a city that welcome newcomers,” she said (Global News). “I will never stop listening, never stop fighting, never stop caring for the people of our city” (CBC News).
  • Bradford conceded: “She is the mayor of this city. Nobody can doubt that” (CTV News).
  • Chow ran on affordability, with promises to freeze TTC fares and make transit free after 10 paid rides a week (CTV News).
  • Loomis, former head of the Hamilton Chamber of Commerce, lost to Horwath by about 1,600 votes in 2022 and beat her by more than 35,000 on Monday (CTV News). On Tuesday Horwath said voters wanted change and that she had congratulated Loomis (CHCH News video).
  • Marianne Meed Ward was re-elected in Burlington (CHCH); Steven Del Duca won Vaughan with 81.5 per cent, the city’s website shows (CBC News).
  • Elsewhere: Patrick Brown won a third term in Brampton (insauga); Conny Glenn beat incumbent Bryan Paterson in Kingston (Global News); Michael Eamon won Peterborough (Global News); Fred Francis beat Drew Dilkens in Windsor (CBC News); Berry Vrbanovic won a fourth term in Kitchener (CBC News). Josh Morgan was re-elected in London (CBC News); four Waterloo region mayors lost, including Waterloo’s Dorothy McCabe and Cambridge’s Jan Liggett (CBC News).
  • Mississauga: the city puts unofficial turnout at 33.9 per cent, up from 21.8 per cent in 2022, and every incumbent councillor who ran was re-elected (insauga). Ottawa: Mark Sutcliffe won 44.65 per cent and beat Jeff Leiper by 33,535 votes; only three new councillors were elected (CBC News).
