## Breaking lasts one hour after the latest news, then Developing (Hannah's rule)
A story in the top slot is "Breaking" only for one hour after its latest real development; after that it automatically becomes "Developing" (calmer navy colours) until the next development, which makes it Breaking again for an hour (BREAKING_PACE_MIN = 60 in the page script). A scheduled update (news conference, vote, ruling) does NOT keep it Breaking on its own; it still shows in #bkNext as "Coming up live". The page does the switching by itself from section#breaking's attributes:
- data-lastdev: ISO Eastern time of the newest real development in the story (a new fact, not a new photo or a reaction quote). Every run, set it to the time of the newest development you add to "How this story developed". Never move it later than the real development.
- data-expected: ISO Eastern time of a scheduled update reported by a reliable outlet. Set it when one is announced (and add it to COMING with top: true; see live rule 3b). It drives "Coming up live", not the Breaking label.
When a new story takes the top slot, set data-since (time of first report), data-lastdev and, if known, data-expected. Elsewhere (wire kickers, card kickers, new log "kind" labels) use "Breaking" only within an hour of the story's latest development; otherwise "Developing". If a bigger story breaks (Canadian or American), it can replace the top slot; move the old one to the wire.

> **How the top two are ordered (Wed Oct 28, 2026).** pick_by_canada(): stories from abroad with no Canadian tie last; everything else by weight = the group's score (newsrooms, National Newswatch, CBC front) + 2 × most-read score + independent newsrooms (max 10) + CANADIAN_WEIGHT (8) for a Canadian story (+4 for a Breaking U.S. election story). Most-read matching folds possessives and matches on a shared name or Canadian place plus one more word.

> **A Canadian event live now leads (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026: von der Leyen spoke live in Montreal while the top was elsewhere).** autopilot.promote_live(), after live_video(): a live stream on air now (or due within 3 hours) that is Canadian or belongs to a Canadian running theme, and matches a top-story candidate, lifts that story (+20 and Breaking when on air, +8 when coming up) and gives it s.live, shown at the top of the story as "▶ Live now on <network>: <what>" with the stream's picture and the other places carrying it. Routine streams (Question Period, 24/7 channels, spacewalks) don't count. A theme kept whole (Canada and the EU) only keeps reports that name the event, so a story that merely shares a city (a Montreal North murder) can't become its headline.

> **"Connected to this story" must really connect (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026).** autopilot.related(): a story in a running theme kept whole (Canada and the EU) lists only other reports about that theme (both sides named), never its own reports. For other stories, a place (city, province, country: is_place()) is never a tie by itself; a connected headline must name a person, organization or issue from the story and share at least one more of its names or key words (connected()).

> **Every live video on a story is on it (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026: "there are live videos that should be attached to the canada eu summit").** promote_live() attaches every matching stream, not one: the first (on air now, else soonest) is the live bar, the rest are listed under it as "More live video on this story". A stream belongs to a running theme when it names the theme's other side (EU, von der Leyen, Costa) plus Canada, a Canadian leader or a Canadian place; other streams are matched against all of a story's reports. The day's uploads from the networks' official channels on the story (s.videos) are listed too, a finished broadcast marked "RECORDING · the broadcast has ended". The connected box never repeats the story's own headline from another newsroom.
> Videos on a top story come from every upload in the last 24 hours on the networks' official YouTube channels (UPLOADS, not just the 2 per network Watch shows) and every stream that ended in the last 12 hours (ENDED, shown as recordings), each checked to play.

> **"What each newsroom reports" opens and closes (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026).** On the top stories it is a header with a Show/Hide button and arrow; a tap anywhere on the header toggles the list. The first story's list starts open, the second's closed, and each reader's choice is kept per story through the page's own 10-minute refreshes (repsOpen in index.html).

> **Independent means independent (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026: "Some are using the same CP story. Others are using the same CTV story ... That would not mean 8 independent sources").** autopilot.report_groups(): two reports are one when the page's own byline/author names the same wire (wire_of_page: The Canadian Press, AP, Reuters, AFP, Bloomberg News), when the outlets share an owner (OWNERS: Bell = CTV/CP24/BNN, Postmedia, Corus, Rogers ...), when the headlines match, or when their opening sentences mostly match. The count of independent reports is the number of groups; the page lists one line per group with "Also run by …". A story is never grouped on a city alone (core_rows ignores places), so separate Toronto stories can't become one.
> Repeats (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026): in every report list (top stories, lead slot, Breaking "Newest reports"), a group of copies is shown once, led by the report POSTED FIRST, with the rest under "Also run by". A primary source (pm.gc.ca, canada.ca and other government sites, the White House, the EU, a newswire release: autopilot.is_primary) always comes first, marked "Primary source". A page whose byline lists the newsroom's own journalists (CTV's live page) is the newsroom's own report even with wire material in it; a wire reporter's name under a newsroom's banner (David Baxter on Global News) counts as that wire.

> **The brief is always in Hannah's voice (Thu Oct 29, 2026: "why does my 60 second brief sound like a robot?").** Editions: morning (to noon), afternoon (to 5 p.m.), evening (to 9 p.m.), night. Until it lands the page plays her most recent recording from the last 12 hours, with a note that the new edition is being recorded; the device's reading voice is only a last resort. > **The brief should sound like Hannah on air (Thu Oct 29, 2026: "It still doesn't sound like my voice").** Tests showed the voice model already matches her tone; what sounded wrong was the delivery. So: each line is a spoken sentence from the newsroom's own opening line (not a headline), credited at the end like a newsreader ("…, The Canadian Press reports."), to the wire when it is a wire story, from its newest copy (autopilot.spoken_line); teasers and live-blog headlines are never read. tools/brief_voice.py uses her steadiest ~10 seconds (best_window), records in passages of 2–3 lines (passages), exaggeration 0.5 / cfg 0.3, and levels the result.

> **Only news becomes Breaking (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026: "what is news nation and why did you decide it should be breaking?").** Talk shows, panels, "debate with …", previews and question headlines are never live coverage (autopilot.COMMENTARY). "Debate" is an event only as a leaders'/candidates'/election debate. A scheduled stream becomes the Breaking event only when it is an editor's own COMING entry, the Prime Minister's media event, a Canadian official event (news conference, statement, announcement, results; not routine Question Period), or the live event of a top story (x.breaking). Other streams are listed under "Coming up live" only.
> "Connected to this story" has a Show/Hide button like "What each newsroom reports"; the reader's choice is kept per story.

> **The newest development leads (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026: "If the newest report is on the Canada EU summit why is Carney Cleveland-Cliffs on top?").** pick_by_canada() weight = group score (newsrooms, National Newswatch, CBC front) + most-read (capped at 8) + independent reports (max 10) + half the newsrooms carrying it (max 6) + freshness (newest report < 1 h +6, < 3 h +3, 6 h or more -4) + 8 for a Canadian story (+4 Breaking U.S. election, +20/+8 Canadian live event). When the top two are within 8 points, the one with the newer report goes first.

> **Canadian sections stand out (Hannah, Thu Oct 29, 2026).** markCanada() in index.html marks a section Canadian when it is Canadian by design (CA_ALWAYS: the Canada desk, Politics, Smaller newsrooms, Canada and the U.S., Most read) or when 60%+ of its links (at least 4) go to Canadian newsrooms or governments (.ca sites and the Canadian .com newsrooms in CA_COM). Marked: a red maple leaf by the title, a Canada-red rule, a faint red tint. A top story that actually names Canada, a Canadian place, leader or institution gets a red "Canada" label. It reruns whenever the page redraws a section. A story naming nothing Canadian is "general", never "canadian", so it gets no Canadian ranking edge.
