Help

How Palindrome works, in plain words.

What happens every 30 minutes?

Five AI editors work in turn, each following the brief you wrote:

  1. Discovery reads the news sites and groups articles about the same event.
  2. Verification reads the articles and counts how many independent newsrooms confirm the story. A wire story carried by six outlets counts as one.
  3. Managing editor ranks the stories by how much they matter to Canadians, not by clicks.
  4. Senior writer writes a short summary from the confirmed facts only.
  5. Standards editor checks every fact, number and link. Only stories it approves can go live.

Overnight (11 p.m. to 6 a.m.), this happens once an hour.

Why is a story "waiting for sources"?

Palindrome only publishes a full summary when at least six independent newsrooms have reported a story. Until then, the site shows just the headline and links, under Developing. It updates on its own as more outlets report.

What does "sent back" mean?

The standards editor found a problem, such as a fact no source supports, wording too close to an article, or a wrong link. The writer gets one try to fix it. If it's still not right, it waits here for you. The reasons are listed on the story.

Can I change where a story appears?

Yes. On any story, you can keep it at the top, move it to a spot, hide it, or change its topic or section. You'll be asked for a reason each time, and it's saved in the history.

Do sponsors affect the ranking?

No. Sponsors never enter the newsroom. The ranking only looks at the news.

Why can't it read some sites?

Some sites need a subscription, block automated readers, or ask not to be read. Palindrome respects that and never gets around it. Those sites are listed on the Today screen.

Something looks wrong. What do I do?

Hide the story right away from the Stories tab, and give a reason. Then check the sources listed on it. If the problem keeps happening, switch off "Publish approved stories automatically" in Settings, so nothing goes live until you tap Publish.
