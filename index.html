Two oil tankers on fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says

First reported by Al Arabiya · Saturday 4:45 p.m. ET

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says a second fully loaded supertanker hit a mine less than 24 hours after the first. The newest and fullest report is Al Jazeera’s, this morning. Neither the U.S. military nor the U.K.’s maritime agency has confirmed either ship yet.

What we know

  • The Guard says two tankers, each carrying about two million barrels of crude, hit mines and caught fire. One was left without power after a blast in its engine room.
  • It says both had switched off their tracking systems and used a route Iran had not approved, and that the strait is closed.
  • Attacks on ships near Hormuz are at a wartime high: six were hit last Sunday, the most in one day since the war began.

Not yet known

  • The ships’ names, flags and owners.
  • Whether anyone was hurt.
  • Independent confirmation from the U.S. military or the U.K.’s maritime agency.

Two accounts

Iran's Revolutionary Guard

The strait is "closed." Two tankers on unapproved routes hit mines and caught fire.

Via Al Jazeera, Al Arabiya and HNGN

U.S. Central Command

The strait is open. About 97% of ships trying to cross got through without being hit.

Via Fox News. It has not addressed these two tankers.

Confirmation tracker 6 of 6

Reporting: Al Arabiya, Al Jazeera, Euronews, CBS News, NewsNation, CGTN, HNGN and The Times of Israel. Every one relays the Revolutionary Guard, so this stays labelled unconfirmed until a second source, such as the U.S. military or the U.K. maritime agency, backs it up.

  1. Sun 10:40 a.m.The Guard says a second supertanker hit a mine. Headline now goes to Al Jazeera’s newest report. Eight outlets report the claim; still not independently confirmed.

  5. Sat 4:45 p.m.Al Arabiya first reports the explosion, citing Iran's Revolutionary Guard.

      Methodology

      A story gets a full Palindrome summary only when six independent newsrooms have reported it. Outlets with the same owner count as one.

      Until then it runs as developing news, with a meter showing how many outlets have it. Breaking news can run sooner, credited to the outlet that reported it, and is labelled unconfirmed.

      Every summary links to the reporting it draws on, and tapping a story takes you to the original article.
