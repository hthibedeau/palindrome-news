What we know

  • Four service members died, Carney said in Ottawa on Sunday, and their next of kin have been notified. Dozens more were seriously hurt. Those affected came from bases across the country, including reservists and soldiers of Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, who were training when the fire broke out. Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland said the unit hit was Charlie Company of the 3rd Battalion, PPCLI: most of the 84-member company was throwing hand grenades on a range while 10 soldiers carried out a simulated attack when the fire reached them (The Canadian Press, via CTV News).
  • National Defence has named the four who died: Sgt. Morgan Wong of the Loyal Edmonton Regiment, serving with 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, and Pte. Shaun Farrell, Pte. Aidan Goulding and Pte. Hayden Lykkemark of 3rd Battalion, PPCLI, based in Edmonton (CTV News; The Globe and Mail, for subscribers). The military says Wong was from Edmonton, Goulding from North Vancouver, Farrell from Prince Albert, Sask., and Lykkemark from Langley, B.C. (CBC News)
  • National Defence says there is no sign of terrorism; the incident is under investigation. Flags on federal buildings are at half-mast.
  • The fire started around 4 p.m. Mountain time (6 p.m. Eastern) Saturday in a training area at the base, about 200 km southeast of Edmonton. Gusts near 100 km/h pushed it past the base fence.
  • Defence Minister David McGuinty confirmed deaths and injuries and said he could not recall an incident like it in recent memory. On Sunday he said he was heading to Edmonton for a briefing. Federal and provincial officials, including McGuinty, Emergency Management Minister Eleanor Olszewski and Premier Smith, began a briefing at 3 p.m. Mountain time (5 p.m. Eastern) Sunday.
  • Premier Smith said Sunday that 39 patients are in hospital, at least 19 in critical condition, at the University of Alberta and Royal Alexandra hospitals in Edmonton, Calgary’s Foothills Medical Centre and the Red Deer Regional Hospital Centre. No more patients are waiting to be moved, and the health system was never overwhelmed, she said. Several hospitals, including Edmonton’s Royal Alexandra and University of Alberta hospitals, went to Code Orange, a mass-casualty alert; Acute Care Alberta said Sunday afternoon that all Code Orange alerts have been stood down. Of the 39 injured, 19 were in critical condition, eight were stable and 12 were walking wounded who did not need to be admitted, and some with severe burns may need weeks or months of specialized treatment; families looking for information can call Health Link at 811 (CKOM). Emergency Health Services Alberta sent 30 units, including 22 ground ambulances and eight air ambulances, in a response that ran more than 10 hours.
  • By late Saturday, the Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire was being held. No evacuation order was issued. An internal military memo obtained by Global News says the fire jumped a fire guard and spread off the base, forcing the evacuation of one family home, and that it is contained but still burning. On Monday afternoon, Wainwright fire chief Aime Smyl said the fire is contained within the municipal district boundary and is not expected to pass the fire guard (CBC News).
  • The Canadian Forces National Investigation Service is leading a full investigation with the support of the RCMP, and 50 investigators are on the ground, Carney told the House of Commons on Monday (CBC News). Emergency Management Minister Eleanor Olszewski said Monday evening that supports for the injured will include some form of “financial compensation” (CBC News).
  • The fire closed advance voting in the area early for Monday’s Alberta referendum; ballots were moved securely. Elections Alberta says voting day goes ahead as planned in Wainwright at the Communiplex and Royal Canadian Legion Branch 29 (CBC News).
  • Three investigations are under way, Gen. Carignan says: the Canadian Forces National Investigation Service with the military police, Alberta’s chief medical examiner and the RCMP; the Canadian Forces Fire Marshal; and a board of inquiry, whose findings will be made public. She plans to visit the base before the end of the week (CTV News).

Not yet known

  • Why the toll of four differs from the earlier reports of at least seven, which cited unnamed military sources.
  • The cause. Military sources told Global News on Sunday that a preliminary investigation suggests a munition from a live training exercise sparked a fast-moving grass fire that led to the explosion (earlier, the same sources spoke of a gas line or well; Alberta’s energy regulator said Sunday it has received no reports of incidents involving oil and gas infrastructure on the base, CBC News reports). Officials have not confirmed this, and it is not clear why live-fire training went ahead under a high-wind warning, Global News reports. Early internal military reports obtained by The Globe and Mail say it is still unclear what ignited the grass fire, and that so far there is no indication ammunition or oil and gas infrastructure was involved.
  • Exactly how many people were hurt. An internal military memo obtained by Global News lists 31 soldiers with “very serious or serious” injuries and six to eight with minor injuries, and cautions the numbers may change. McGuinty would not say whether weapons or ammunition were involved.
  • When the cause will be known: investigators say it could be weeks before details of how the fire started can be determined (Global News).
  • When the Canadian Armed Forces’ board of inquiry will report. Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland said the military will hold one, and that with “this amount of casualties and complexities, it’s not going to take a day or a week” (CTV News). Chief of the Defence Staff Gen. Jennie Carignan has vowed to release the results of the official probe (The Globe and Mail, for subscribers). An Oro-Medonte, Ont., mayoral candidate, Cathy Keane, says her eldest son is among the critically injured (CTV News). She has stopped door-knocking to be with her son but remains in the race: “As a mother, right now I need to be with my son and family” (CBC News).
