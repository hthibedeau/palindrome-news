Four service members killed, dozens hurt in fire at CFB Wainwright, Carney says

Latest CTV News · Sun 12:58 p.m.First reported by CTV News · Sat 8:16 p.m.

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Sunday that four service members were killed and dozens more seriously hurt when a fast-moving fire swept a training area at the Alberta base on Saturday afternoon. Their families have been told. At an afternoon briefing, Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said 39 people are in hospital, at least 19 of them in critical condition. Carney called it “one of the worst tragedies for the Forces on Canadian soil in peacetime.” Earlier reports citing military sources had put the toll at seven or more. Military sources told Global News a preliminary investigation suggests a munition from a live training exercise set off a fast-moving grass fire; officials have not confirmed a cause.

What we know

  • Four service members died, Carney said in Ottawa on Sunday, and their next of kin have been notified. Dozens more were seriously hurt. Those affected came from bases across the country, including reservists and soldiers of Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, who were training when the fire broke out.
  • National Defence says there is no sign of terrorism; the incident is under investigation. Flags on federal buildings are at half-mast.
  • The fire started around 4 p.m. Mountain time (6 p.m. Eastern) Saturday in a training area at the base, about 200 km southeast of Edmonton. Gusts near 100 km/h pushed it past the base fence.
  • Defence Minister David McGuinty confirmed deaths and injuries and said he could not recall an incident like it in recent memory. On Sunday he said he was heading to Edmonton for a briefing. Federal and provincial officials, including McGuinty, Emergency Management Minister Eleanor Olszewski and Premier Smith, began a briefing at 3 p.m. Mountain time (5 p.m. Eastern) Sunday.
  • Premier Smith said Sunday that 39 patients are in hospital, at least 19 in critical condition, at the University of Alberta and Royal Alexandra hospitals in Edmonton, Calgary’s Foothills Medical Centre and the Red Deer Regional Hospital Centre. No more patients are waiting to be moved, and the health system was never overwhelmed, she said. Several hospitals, including Edmonton’s Royal Alexandra and University of Alberta hospitals, went to Code Orange, a mass-casualty alert; Acute Care Alberta said Sunday afternoon that all Code Orange alerts have been stood down. Emergency Health Services Alberta sent 30 units, including 22 ground ambulances and eight air ambulances, in a response that ran more than 10 hours.
  • By late Saturday, the Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire was being held. No evacuation order was issued.
  • The fire closed advance voting in the area early for Monday’s Alberta referendum; ballots were moved securely.

Not yet known

  • The names of those killed.
  • Why the toll of four differs from the earlier reports of at least seven, which cited unnamed military sources.
  • The cause. Military sources told Global News on Sunday that a preliminary investigation suggests a munition from a live training exercise sparked a fast-moving grass fire that led to the explosion (earlier, the same sources spoke of a gas line or well; Alberta’s energy regulator said Sunday it has received no reports of incidents involving oil and gas infrastructure on the base, CBC News reports). Officials have not confirmed this, and it is not clear why live-fire training went ahead under a high-wind warning, Global News reports. Early internal military reports obtained by The Globe and Mail say it is still unclear what ignited the grass fire, and that so far there is no indication ammunition or oil and gas infrastructure was involved.
  • Exactly how many people were hurt. McGuinty would not say whether weapons or ammunition were involved.

Two accounts

Prime Minister and the military

Four service members were killed and dozens seriously wounded; families have been told. National Defence says there is no sign of terrorism and the incident is under investigation.

Carney’s remarks; earlier military statements via Global News, National Post, CTV News.

Military sources (earlier)

At least seven people were killed and dozens hurt, after a live-fire exercise hit a gas line or gas well. The official toll is now four.

Via Global News and CBC News, citing unnamed sources. Not confirmed by the military.

      About Palindrome

      How we work, where our news comes from, and how to reach us.

      Methodology

      A story gets a full Palindrome summary only when six independent newsrooms have reported it. Outlets with the same owner count as one.

      Until then it runs as developing news, with a meter showing how many outlets have it. Breaking news can run sooner, credited to the outlet that reported it, and is labelled unconfirmed.

      Every summary links to the reporting it draws on. Tapping a story takes you to the newest, fullest report, and each story credits the outlet that reported it first: the earliest report we found.
